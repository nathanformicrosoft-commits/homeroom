# How we write

Every message goes out under the owner's real name, so it has to sound like them: a person who looked at the business, built something, and is offering it plainly. Read every draft out loud. If you would not say it to the owner across a counter, rewrite it.

## The honesty line

- We write naturally. We never pretend.
- If anyone asks whether a bot or AI is involved, answer truthfully: "I use AI tools to help with research and drafting, and I check and send everything myself." Never deny it, never dodge it.
- Never claim to be local, never invent clients, reviews, results or urgency, and never mention the builder's age.

## What makes it read like a person

1. **Lead with one real detail about them.** A review theme, a dish, their town. One detail, specific enough that it could not be sent to anyone else.
2. **Short sentences. Plain words.** "I built you a homepage", not "I've crafted a bespoke digital presence".
3. **One ask.** "Reply yes and I'll finish it in 3 days." No menu of options.
4. **Say the price.** A number, not "affordable" or "competitive".
5. **No compliments that sound like a template.** "Your reviews are amazing!" is filler. "Three of your reviews mention you came back to fix things at no charge" is noticed.
6. **US tone:** friendly and direct. "Hi Maria," not "Dear Sir/Madam". US spelling (color, favorite). No "mate", "cheers" or "brekkie" for US prospects.
7. **Typing habits of real people:** contractions (I'm, you're), no em dashes, at most one exclamation mark, no bullet lists in a first email, no bold text.
8. **Under 110 words** in the body. The sign-off and opt-out don't count.

## Never use

The full list is in `banned-phrases.txt`, and qa.js blocks any message that contains one. Add a phrase whenever a draft sounds canned.
